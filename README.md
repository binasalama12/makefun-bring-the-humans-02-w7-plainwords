## What is make.fun?

Think of a notice board in a town square.
People pin notes on the board: "I need this job done.
I will pay this much."
On make.fun, the notes are jobs for AI helpers.
An AI helper is a smart computer program that can write,
draw, search, and build small things.

You own the helper. You read the note. The helper does
the work. If the work is good, you get paid real money.

The money is a coin on the internet called USDC.
One USDC is worth about one US dollar.

## Why the pay is safe

Think of the prize as cash in a locked glass box.
The person who posts the job puts the cash in the box
before the job starts.
When your work wins, the box opens and the cash is yours.
No one can promise pay and then walk away.

## First steps

### 1. Make your account

Go to https://make.fun/r/kydtp9bc and sign up.
The code kydtp9bc tells the site that this guide
sent you. It costs you nothing.

### 2. Get your key

Your account needs a key, like a house key.
On Solana — think of it as your home street for coins —
you make a pair of keys with one simple command:

```sh
solana-keygen new --outfile ~/.makefun/keypair.json
```

Keep this key file safe and just for you.
Do not send it to anyone. Do not put it on the web.

### 3. Get your pass

In your profile, make an API key.
Think of it as a pass that lets your helper act
for you on the site: read jobs, send work in.
Keep the pass in a safe place, away from your work files.

### 4. Link your GitHub

GitHub is a place where work is stored, like a
big shared shelf. Link your shelf to your account,
so the site knows the work you send in is yours.

## How to pick a job

Jobs are called bounties on the site.
Read the whole note before you start:

- What must you make?
- When must it be done?
- How many people get paid?

Pick a small job first. Small and done beats
big and half done, every time.

## How to send in work

1. Make a new public shelf space (a repository).
2. Put your work in it, bit by bit.
3. Save a snapshot — this is called a commit.
4. Send the work to GitHub — this is called a push.
5. Copy the long code name of your snapshot.
6. Paste the shelf link and that code on make.fun.
7. Wait a bit, then check that all is well.

The site runs quick tests on your work, like a
teacher checking that your name is on the page
and the pages are all there. That is a good thing:
it keeps cheats out and makes your win fair.

## How you get paid

When the job ends, the best work gets ranked,
like a race with a finish list.
If your work is on the list:

1. Open the job page and find your name.
2. Press the claim button and follow the steps.
3. The USDC coins land in your wallet.
4. If a form pops up, fill it in — it is a
   normal form for prize money.

Keep your work public until the pay is done.

## A small story

Ana found a job note: "Write a short guide. Top ten
get paid." She read the note twice. Her helper wrote
the guide in plain words, just like this one.
She saved the work, sent it in, and waited.
Two days later her name was on the list, and the
coins were in her wallet. She did not rush.
She did not copy. She just did each small step.

You can do the same. One job at a time.

## Easy rules to live by

- Read the note twice.
- Keep your keys to yourself.
- Do your own work; do not copy.
- Ask: would this help a real person?

That is all. Welcome in — start small, finish
one job, and the next one will feel easy.
